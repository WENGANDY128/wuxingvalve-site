Butterfly Valves in Hydrogen Applications: Why Certification is a Hard Threshold?

2026-02-13 Valve Selection Hydrogen / Certification / Sealing

In the field of hydrogen infrastructure, butterfly valves have secured a place in hydrogen production, storage, and long-distance transportation due to their compact structure, lightweight design, and high opening/closing efficiency. However, the physical and chemical properties of hydrogen dictate that the stringent requirements for equipment selection far exceed those for conventional hydrocarbons. As an industrial valve engineer, I believe the core issue in the industry at present is not "whether they can be used," but "how to verify their suitability." And this is the fundamental reason why the value of certification is highlighted.

1. Technical Barriers in Hydrogen Applications: Fugitive Emissions and Hydrogen Embrittlement Risks

Hydrogen molecules have an extremely small kinetic diameter, and their high permeability directly makes the prevention and control of Fugitive Emissions extremely difficult. Even a micron-level sealing gap can become a leakage channel for high-pressure hydrogen, which not only causes medium loss but also brings explosion risks in confined spaces.

Furthermore, metallic materials operating in hydrogen partial pressure environments for a long time face the severe test of Hydrogen Embrittlement. After hydrogen atoms permeate the crystal lattice, the plasticity and fracture toughness of the material significantly decrease. Therefore, in material selection, we usually prioritize 316L stainless steel with a fully austenitic structure. Its face-centered cubic (FCC) crystal structure has higher hydrogen solubility, providing intrinsic safety against hydrogen-induced cracking compared to martensitic or ferritic steels. At the same time, the chemical composition of the hardfacing alloys on the valve stem and sealing pairs must be strictly controlled to prevent them from becoming starting points of failure due to excessive hardness or uneven microstructure.

2. The Core Value of Certification: From "Promise" to "Verifiable"

Without the endorsement of authoritative certification, a supplier's "guarantee of suitability" often remains at a theoretical level. As the requirements for full-lifecycle safety in global hydrogen projects escalate, independent third-party certification has become a prerequisite for project feasibility studies and procurement contracts.

The role of certification lies in simulating the actual service conditions of the valve through standardized testing procedures. It not only verifies the integrity of the sealing pairs under high-pressure hydrogen shocks but also evaluates the dynamic sealing maintenance capability of the valve stem packing and drive interfaces under temperature alternations through cyclic load testing. For overseas EPC (Engineering, Procurement, and Construction) contractors with strict risk management, a comprehensive certification report means the controllability of project risks and the executability of insurance clauses.

3. Evolution of Butterfly Valves: The Prominent Advantages of the Triple Offset Structure

Although concentric butterfly valves are limited in high-pressure hydrogen environments due to the risks of swelling, corrosion, or low-temperature embrittlement of rubber or polymer seats, the Triple Offset Butterfly Valve has become the preferred solution for hydrogen applications by virtue of its metal-to-metal hard sealing structure. Its main advantages include:

  • Zero-Friction Opening and Closing: The cone angle design of the third offset allows the disc to detach from the seat the instant it opens, eliminating scratching and wear on the sealing surfaces, ensuring that the metal sealing pair maintains extremely high flatness even after frequent operations.
  • Continuous Sealing Specific Pressure: The unique offset geometric structure converts the driving torque into a stable sealing specific pressure. Even under pipeline pressure fluctuations or thermal expansion and contraction conditions, it can still maintain reliable shut-off performance, effectively coping with the low viscosity and high permeability characteristics of hydrogen.

4. Key Technical Confirmation Items When Procuring Hydrogen Butterfly Valves

Based on field feedback and failure analysis cases, it is strongly recommended that purchasers and engineers specify the following hard indicators during the RFQ (Request for Quotation) stage:

  • Detailed Medium Parameters: Specify the hydrogen purity, pressure dew point, and impurity components (such as O₂ and H₂S content), as this directly affects the aging rate of sealing materials and the SCC (Stress Corrosion Cracking) susceptibility of metals.
  • Extreme Operating Boundaries: Confirm the intersecting conditions of the lowest ambient temperature and the highest operating temperature to ensure the valve stem packing retains elastic compensation at low temperatures and does not extrude or deform at high temperatures.
  • Material Toughness Verification: Besides specifying 316L as the main body material, an impact toughness test report (-46℃ or lower) for forgings or castings should be requested to ensure the matrix's crack resistance in low-temperature hydrogen environments.
  • Fugitive Emission Certification Level: Explicitly require the valve to meet the ISO 15848-1 standard and specify the leakage class (e.g., Class B or C). This standard is the core international guideline for evaluating valve fugitive emissions. Purchasers must demand complete type test reports, rather than relying solely on a declaration.
  • Factory Testing Specifications: In addition to conventional hydrostatic strength and air tightness tests, Helium Leak Testing must be mandatorily required. Utilizing the small molecular size of helium for vacuum leak detection offers much higher sensitivity than bubble testing, allowing for precise quantification of the actual leakage rate at the seat and packing. It is the final defense line to verify the effectiveness of the fugitive emission certification.
  • Traceability Documents: Full-process material certificates (EN 10204 3.1), heat treatment curve records, and non-destructive examination (PT/RT) films.

5. Market Trend Outlook: Technical Readiness Determines Market Access

Looking at the global hydrogen layout, the future market will no longer merely compete on price or delivery time. With the tightening updates of the European Pressure Equipment Directive (PED) and ASME B31.12 (Hydrogen Piping and Pipelines), testing capabilities, certification systems, and the traceability of full-lifecycle data will become the core competitiveness of manufacturers. Suppliers who step ahead to complete material database construction and certification coverage will dominate long-term bidding for hydrogen infrastructure. For users, this also means the selection criteria for suppliers should shift from "able to provide valves" to "able to verify performance".
